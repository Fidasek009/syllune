# Product principles

Build Syllune, a Firefox and Chrome extension that makes listening to a web page feel effortless. Microsoft Edge's Read Aloud is the experience benchmark; Piper Read Aloud demonstrates the user's acceptable voice quality, but its UI/UX is a problem to solve.

## Brand and marketing

- Lead with **Syllune**. The store title is **Syllune: Offline Text to Speech**. Use `https://github.com/Fidasek009/syllune` for repository links. Keep existing extension IDs, storage keys and release archive names stable. Do not include obsolete product names or naming history in public copy.
- Lead public copy with **Natural-sounding voices. On your computer.** State voice naturalness beside local processing in headlines, opening descriptions and promotional artwork; do not leave it implicit or bury it in technical details. Supporting copy must explain that speech works offline after an explicit voice download and reading text never goes to a speech service. Online documents and streamed samples still need the network. Never imply zero network requests or offline model provisioning.
- Make natural speech and an effortless, premium reading experience the product's identity. Demonstrate voices through the product's actual samples and listening through real playback captures. "Natural-sounding" describes the product, not a market ranking or a guarantee about every voice variant. Do not call competitors robotic or claim best, most natural, fastest, exact timing or universal compatibility without dated evidence that supports the comparison.
- Use the orange S icon and Syllune wordmark consistently. Show reading before voice management in marketing. Keep promotional claims out of the playback bar and do not add onboarding steps, review nags, telemetry or permissions for marketing.
- App colors come from `src/theme.css`: warm backgrounds, charcoal text and orange actions with dark labels. Share light/dark colors across voices, playback and PDF controls. Keep errors/Delete red, system fonts and document content unchanged.
- Use the unslop skill whenever writing or editing prose, code, comments or documentation. Keep copy concrete and direct. No invented reviews, endorsements, rankings, scarcity, keyword stuffing or competitor impersonation. External promotion and store changes require authorization.
- `store/listing.md` owns store copy, launch drafts and the dated positioning evidence. Keep README, manifest copy and public privacy materials consistent with it. Acceptance and license limits still apply.

## Non-negotiable values

1. **Brutal simplicity.** Every feature, control, setting, permission, and onboarding step must earn its place. Prefer a good default over another choice. Do not add functionality just because the engine supports it.
2. **Premium means effortless.** Prioritize predictable behavior, clear state, responsive controls, polished details, and seamless browser integration. Visual decoration and more settings do not constitute quality.
3. **Natural speech is a core requirement.** US English and Czech are mandatory. Make all additional languages supported by the vetted model/runtime combination available without unnecessary UI. Piper medium-quality speech is an acceptable baseline; let users trade quality for speed through their model choice. Do not treat robotic speech as an acceptable way to claim the requirement is finished.
4. **Speech runs in the extension on the user's machine.** No separately installed helper app, cloud synthesis, or sending page text/audio to remote inference services. Use GPU acceleration when available and working, with a usable local CPU fallback. Validate both paths; an API being present is not proof a model works well on that device.
5. **The reading experience comes first.** Optimize the path from wanting to listen to hearing the right text. Reading order, excluding unrelated page content, stopping promptly, and recovering gracefully matter as much as synthesis quality.
6. **Firefox and Chrome are first-class.** Share implementation where sensible, but verify behavior and lifecycle on both browsers. Do not assume Chromium-only APIs or WebGPU support work in Firefox.
7. **Accessibility is part of polish.** All controls must work with keyboard and assistive technology, have meaningful accessible names, visible focus, and appropriate contrast. Respect reduced motion and avoid unnecessary animation.
8. **Privacy and trust by design.** Request the minimum permissions needed for agreed behavior. Keep reading content out of logs and analytics. Clearly explain necessary model downloads and local storage. Never disguise a network dependency as offline capability.
9. **Performance is user experience.** Treat startup time, time to first audio, continuity, cancellation, battery use, memory, model size, and download friction as product concerns. Measure on representative hardware, including CPU-only devices.
10. **Current, verified engineering.** Use maintained dependencies and current official browser, extension-store, security, and accessibility guidance. Date architecture research and cite primary sources. Verify claims against information available in 2026; do not invent a universal "2026 standard" or equate newest with best.

## Confirmed product requirements

- Target Firefox and Chrome on **Windows and Linux**. Design for **4 CPU cores and 8 GB RAM**; a GPU with **4 GB VRAM is optional**, not required.
- Initial reading surfaces: **web articles, explicitly selected text, and PDFs including the supplied thesis**. The essential interaction is **select text → right-click → read that selection nearly instantly** once provisioned. Do not silently continue beyond the selection. All three user benchmark URLs in `docs/product-brief.md` must work in the first release.
- **Play in an existing overlay reads the current text selection when one exists**, replacing the previous reading scope. Without a new selection, resume or replay that scope. Navigation and completion must stay within the selected text, on web pages and in our PDF reader.
- **Medium-model latency goal:** no more than **1 second warm** and **2 seconds cold** on the target 4-core machine. Measure them separately and improve further when possible. The owner explicitly waived performance as a first-release blocker on 2026-11-06; retain honest measurements and track optimization after release.
- **Open PDF reading in a new extension reader tab**, preserving the original PDF tab. Keep the same playback bar and highlighting.
- **An already-open local PDF must use its known file URL when browser permissions allow.** Do not force the user to choose the same file again as the default path. Request only necessary browser access; if the browser blocks direct reading, state that limitation honestly rather than claiming seamless support.
- Provide a **speed slider that preserves vocal pitch**. Pitching voices upward to make playback faster is unacceptable. Generate speech at normal model speed and apply speed changes to playback without restarting synthesis. Preserve pitch and validate audio quality at changed speeds.
- Provide an **Edge-like playback bar**, **word-by-word highlighting**, and **automatic scrolling**. Use accurate playback synchronization; do not claim exact alignment from arbitrary timers.
- Keep **play/pause, previous/next sentence, speed, volume, close/stop, and voice choice together** in that playback surface. Match Edge's interaction as closely as public extension APIs permit. **Do not add sentence clicking** to the first release.
- Bind keyboard/headset media play/pause to playback and previous/next track to sentence navigation through the browser's Media Session API. Release media controls when the reader closes.
- **Continue reading across tab switches; stop on source-page navigation or source-tab closure.** Closing the playback controls also intentionally stops. Keep playback independent of popup lifetime, and prevent stale jobs from restarting stopped sessions.
- **Never download a model automatically.** Users explicitly choose and download models; download size itself is not a limiting requirement. Let them **delete downloaded models reliably** and choose between quality/performance variants.
- **Minimize extension size. Do not bundle voice previews.** Stream prerecorded samples only after an explicit Listen action; do not prefetch them or download a model for auditioning.
- Offer voice auditioning, preferably with **prerecorded samples**, without requiring a full model download. Fetch a preview only in response to the user's action, and make network/storage behavior clear.
- License this project's original source code under **MIT**. Audit runtime, phonemizer, model, data, and preview licenses separately. A permissive wrapper or model label does not establish the license of bundled third-party binaries. Do not silently impose an incompatible distribution license.
- The user accepts considering separately licensed dependencies, provided their obligations are explained **before choosing** them. Do not mislabel third-party code or models as MIT or imply that notices alone satisfy all licenses.
- **Manage the full voice catalog uniformly**, including every language and quality variant (currently 178 upstream candidates; 171 available models). There is no two-model scope or separate English/Czech implementation path. Preview production and coverage must derive from the shared catalog. Exclude unsupported engines and demonstrated upstream/runtime incompatibilities from download and reading choices; diagnose and fix our integration errors before excluding models. Preserve deletion of older downloads. Keep runtime and license limitations explicit.
- Manual voice/language choice is sufficient for the first release. Do not add automatic detection unless it is demonstrably simple and reliable.
- **Use WXT with strict TypeScript**, following WXT's official project structure and TypeScript guidance, plus Mozilla's extension UX/security/publishing guidance and Chrome's extension requirements. Build Manifest V3 explicitly for both browsers. Keep page and worker environment types explicit; type-check message contracts and state transitions. Third-party reference code may retain its upstream language and license.
- **Bun is the sole project runtime and toolchain.** Use Bun for installs, scripts, tests, native type checking (`bun check`), formatting (`bun fmt`), and any standalone bundling (`Bun.build`). Run the complete checking and formatting gates with `bun run typecheck` and `bun run format:check`; they cover the separate environments. Run WXT and its build tooling under Bun. Keep one `bun.lock`; do not introduce Node runtime requirements, package-lock files, or npm/npx commands. Prefer Bun-native APIs in first-party tooling. Check current Bun release notes and official docs before adding replacement packages for capabilities Bun already provides.

## Working rules

- The implementation agent owns artwork, icons, store screenshots, listing/privacy copy, and release engineering. The product owner does not create these deliverables. A pushed `vX.Y.Z` tag is the sole release-version authority: derive manifests, archives, store submissions, and the automatically published GitHub release from it without a package-version bump or separate release-ready toggle. Account identity, required legal declarations, store review, and existing license/product acceptance obligations remain real constraints.
- The user is the product owner; the agent owns implementation and engineering decisions. Refactor or replace first-party code autonomously when it improves the agreed product. Keep product requirements, privacy, license obligations, and measured acceptance targets intact; do not seek approval for routine engineering choices.
- Keep the initial product scope small. Before adding a feature or UI component, identify the essential user problem it solves and why a simpler existing interaction is insufficient.
- Distinguish user-confirmed requirements from proposals and unanswered questions. Do not silently turn an implementation preference into a product requirement.
- Research and prototype the hard constraints before committing to an engine or framework: local voice quality, browser-compatible inference, CPU performance, GPU compatibility, model licensing, and extension lifecycle.
- Follow each browser's current extension manifest, content security policy, packaging, and store-review requirements. Bundle executable code; verify any model or runtime loading strategy against the actual policies.
- Handle web page content as untrusted input. Keep page extraction, extension control, inference, and playback boundaries explicit; validate messages crossing them.
- Prefer a small, understandable architecture and platform capabilities over unnecessary dependencies. Do not introduce accounts, cloud services, telemetry, monetization, extra reading surfaces, or extensive preferences without an agreed need.
- Reuse maintained libraries and established upstream behavior before writing custom implementations. Keep extension-specific adapters small and share common logic; do not reinvent existing functionality.
- Test meaningful user behavior on both target browsers. Include CPU fallback, interrupted playback, model-download failure, and long-page reading when those paths exist.
- Reproduce browser-specific reports in the reported browser/build. Verify real worker startup, propagate browser script exceptions as test failures, and state when fixtures use simulated entry or test-only permissions.
- Serialize browser-side probe assertions with `scripts/page-script.ts`: keep injected functions self-contained and type-checked instead of embedding hand-written JavaScript strings.
- For UI work, verify keyboard access, accessible state changes, focus behavior, and real-browser layout as well as functionality, using the tools permitted in the current task.
- Batch verification after related changes. Do not rerun broad checks after every small edit; repeat or expand checks only when a failure, new change, or unresolved concern warrants it.
- Record unresolved product decisions and research evidence in project documentation rather than bloating the interface or this file.
- Never claim voice quality, latency, compatibility, offline operation, or policy compliance based only on a library's marketing. Identify what has been measured and what remains unverified.
